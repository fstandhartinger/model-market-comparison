## Regression — the fast-lane banner hides the first result on a phone (new, D207)

Florian's CR-167 request says the banner must be *"fixed at the bottom, readable in light and dark,
and **not covering content on mobile** (reserve space or make it compact)"*. The implementation
reserves space by putting the banner's measured height into `body { padding-bottom }`, which keeps
the **end** of the document reachable. It does nothing for the first viewport, where the banner is
an opaque fixed overlay.

Measured on the live `/jev-models` at 390×844, light and dark, on **both** hosts
(`banner-{canonical,legacy}/{verification,metrics}.json`):

| | value |
|---|---|
| banner height on a phone | **145 px** (17% of the viewport) |
| banner top edge | y **699** |
| first `[data-bh-jev-class-list] > li` | y **699**, height **79 px**, bottom **778** |
| result | the whole first Jev-class row is behind the banner |

So a first-time phone visitor to the flagship page sees **no result at all** until they dismiss the
banner. CR-163 existed precisely to put that row on the first screen, and Kimi verified it at 647 px
at 18:11 UTC; the banner landed at 19:0x.

Two things moved together. CR-166 added a one-line "Making decisions from images? Explore Image
JevBench v0.1…" paragraph to the `/jev-models` head — **+52 px** on a phone, which is why the row is
at 699 rather than 647. CR-167 then put a 145 px overlay at the bottom.

**Why pass-35 still reads 190/190.** Its F-189 check is `first headline row at y ≤ 720`, measured as
a document offset. 699 ≤ 720, so it passes — the check cannot see a fixed overlay. The verifier is
not wrong about what it measures; it measures the wrong thing for this defect. This gate's own
harness checks visibility against the banner's top edge instead, and that is the check that fails.

**Not fixed here, and deliberately not.** No mechanical tweak reaches it. Reclaiming CR-166's 52 px
would put the row back at 647 — its bottom would then be 726, still **27 px** behind a banner top of
699. Clearing a 79 px row needs a banner no taller than **118 px** at y 647, or **66 px** at the
current y; 66 px cannot hold Florian's dictated copy above a 44 px button row. The real options —
materially shorter phone copy, a collapsed teaser, or accepting that a dismissible bar covers the
first result until tapped — are choices between two of Florian's own same-day instructions. That is
the design authority's call, not a review gate's, and reshaping the page to satisfy a budget is
exactly what the directives forbid. Filed as **D207**, open, with the numbers above.

## Consent decision record — its own reopening conditions were met (new, D208)

`ops/ux-2026-09-12/CR-67.5-CONSENT-DECISION.md` §3 lists what reopens the no-banner consent
decision: *"client-side script/beacon, cookie or storage for statistics, any IP-derived or hashed
key (unique visitors), `Accept-CH`, full referrer URLs or query strings, an external analytics
provider, joining statistics with accounts, longer retention."*

CR-167 adds the first of those and arguably the sixth: `components/FastlaneBanner.tsx` calls
`navigator.sendBeacon('/api/fastlane-banner-event')` on view, click and dismiss, and the route
forwards each event to a Umami service on a second host. The decision record is byte-identical in
this range. What did change is the *name* of the test that carried its premise —
`test/visit-stats.test.mjs` went from "the site ships no client analytics, so no consent banner is
needed" to "aggregate visitor counts require no sitewide analytics script", with the assertion body
untouched. The premise moved; the record did not follow it.

To be clear about what the implementation does and does not do, because it is careful: the event
payload is only the event name and which of the two page groups it came from; the endpoint refuses
anything whose `Origin` is not the public origin or whose host is not an allowed public host, caps
the body at 256 bytes, allow-lists the three event names, honours `DNT` and `Sec-GPC` on **both**
the client and the server, forwards the User-Agent but **not** the visitor's IP, and pins a single
Umami origin with `redirect: "error"` and a 4-second timeout. `/privacy` was updated the same day
and describes all of it, including the storage of the dismissal choice. Nothing here looks careless.

The finding is narrower and still real: a compliance record that names its own trigger conditions
was tripped and not revisited, so the shipped position rests on a document that no longer describes
the site. Needs Florian or a fresh decision record. Filed as **D208**, open. One residual note for
whoever writes it: the endpoint is unauthenticated and its `Origin` guard is a browser convention,
so a non-browser client can inflate the three counters. That is an analytics-accuracy matter, not a
privacy one.
