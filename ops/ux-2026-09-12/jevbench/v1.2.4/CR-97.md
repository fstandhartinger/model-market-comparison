## What classifier.dev's own pages say (read 20 Sep 2026, saved with the job's evidence)

- **The fast tier is Jev.** "The fast tier is Jev, TypeSafe's decision model" (classifier.dev/benchmark); "The model is
  Jev, TypeSafe's decision model" (classifier.dev/about); the documented API response is
  `"tier": "fast", "model": "jev-1.13.0"` — the version JevBench measures directly as Jev 1.13.0.
- **The smart tier is escalation on low confidence** — a model cascade, *not* best-of-N, self-consistency, a review
  pass over every answer, or a committee: "The smart tier is Jev plus a reasoning model re-asking only the answers Jev
  put under 0.7 confidence" (classifier.dev/benchmark). It escalates to **gemini-3.8-flash** at "about $0.70 per
  thousand escalated items"; on their own sets it fired on 43 of 400 and 126 of 400 items. **JevBench never ran the
  smart tier** — our row is the fast tier only.
- **Price.** "Free, or Pro for $20/month with 10× classification limits" (classifier.dev/pricing). Free: 20,000 fast
  classifications a day per IP (this is what our run used, and it cost nothing). Pro: 200,000 a day. A "Partner" tier
  exists by arrangement, price not published.
- **Why it is that cheap: they do not say, so neither do we.** Florian's hunch about a free waitlist or Vercel quota is
  **not published anywhere on their site** and is not on our page. (The single mention of Vercel is about transport —
  "`jev@vercel` and `jev-1.x` are the same model asked two ways".) The only cost figure they publish is what the model
  costs a *caller*: "The model behind the fast tier costs about $0.005 per thousand classifications and needs a
  TypeSafe key" — measured on their short single-sentence inputs, not on JevBench's whole questions.

## The change

- **CR-97.1** Artifact = jevbench repo `results/v1.2/` at tag **`v1.2.4`** (`data/raw/benchmarks/jevbench/v1.2/`, SHA
  pinned in `lib/jevbench-v12.mjs`; per-task re-pinned to `ops/ux-2026-09-12/jevbench/v1.2.4/`). Every row now carries a
  `listing` — `ranked`, `honorable_mention` or `partial` — and **only a ranked row carries a rank**. `lib/jevbench-v12.mjs`
  refuses an artifact whose `ranked`/`partial` flags disagree with its `listing`, whose unranked row carries a rank, or
  whose honorable mention does not name a *ranked* system whose model it runs.
- **CR-97.2** The rule is general, not a one-off, and is published with the data
  (`honorable_mentions.rule`, repeated in `scoring.ranked`): *a service that runs another entrant's model is listed
  with all of its scores and axes, but is not ranked against the models.* It appears in the method section, above the
  honorable-mention block in the table, and in the section itself.
- **CR-97.3** New section under the ranking, **"Honorable mentions — services built on another entrant's model"**
  (`#jev12-honorable`): classifier.dev with its score (84.8), all four axes and its price, **no rank number**,
  "Runs on Jev (TypeSafe)", and three short paragraphs — why it is not ranked, the price with its flat-rate caveat, and
  the one honest finding (on our set the fast tier scored 97.3 % on judge items against Jev's 94.5 %, and 70.5 %
  against 74.1 % on hard ones, so it is not simply pass-through). The service is credited, never criticised.
- **CR-97.4** The CR-95.3 headline bullet ("why a service built on Jev leads") is replaced by the reason it no longer
  leads. **Jev 1.13.0 is #1 at 75.4**; SemIf #2, djev #3, and every row below classifier.dev moves up one place. No
  measurement, axis, price or score changed — `main-score.png` and `axes.png` were regenerated from the same numbers.
- **CR-97.5** classifier.dev keeps everything else: the radar picker offers it under "Honorable mentions (not ranked)",
  the per-task grid keeps its column, the topic aggregates keep its row, and its footnote and cost basis are unchanged.
